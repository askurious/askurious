# Other pages

Pages outside a survey: constructs, groups, designs, sample size, account and element types, plus the learning aids.

## 20. Pages outside a survey

### Constructs

![Constructs: validated scales and standard questions, with filters by type, source and topic](img/constructs.jpg)

The pool of validated scales and standard questions (e.g. BFI-10, technology acceptance), searchable by name, author, year and wording, with filters by type, source (e.g. GESIS ZIS, APA PsycTests) and discipline.
Starred constructs appear in the editor's sidebar.
Researchers can suggest new constructs.

**Why.**
Reusing validated instruments is better science than writing new questions, and every survey that uses a construct adds to its pooled reliability across studies.
The pooled data are pseudonymous per study and person, and the terms of use name this use.

### Groups

![Groups: share surveys and designs with a team](img/groups.jpg)

A group is a list of people one works with regularly (a chair, a project).
It shares surveys and designs all at once instead of person by person.
A group owns nothing: anyone who leaves loses only what was shared through the group (decision of 2026-10-01).

### Designs

![Designs of the platform, own designs and shared designs](img/designs.jpg)

Saved appearances for participant pages: designs of the platform (e.g. "Askurious dark", "Paper", "Sage"), own designs and designs shared with the person.
A survey always gets its own copy, so changing a design later changes no running survey.

### Sample size

![The sample size calculator](img/sample-size.jpg)

Found in the menu “Help and tools” of the header, from the research plan and from the start of a survey. A calculator for rough planning: what to compare (two groups, correlation, proportions, …), effect size, α, power, group ratio, one- or two-sided, expected exclusions.
It answers "how many participants do I need?" and can take the result over as the sample target of a survey.

### Account

![Account, page “Profile and sign-in”: details from ORCID, sign-in, sessions](img/account.jpg)

The account has four pages, all in the menu at the name under the heading **Account**; above the title of each of them a small “Account” shows. **Profile and sign-in**: details, access, sign-in and security (password, second factor, sessions), experimental features; “Delete account” comes last. **Connections**: everything with a stored key or token: data access tokens, Zenodo, Coscine, the own AI access and the AI agent interface. **Display and language**: light or dark, style, text size and language of the interface, emails, learning aids. **AI usage**: whether generative AI is used, your own AI calls and the platform's models. Designs and groups stand above in the menu, under “My collections”: designs are for surveys, the display only for your own screen.
Every account knows two ways to sign in: ORCID, and email address with password. Depending on how one joined, one or the other is active first; under **Sign-in** the second can be added. Once connected with ORCID, name and institution come from the public ORCID record; the iD can be disconnected again as long as email address and password are added, and email address and password can be removed as long as ORCID is connected. Surveys, groups and shares belong to the account, not to the way of signing in, and stay. Someone added by ORCID iD before ever signing in appears as “not signed in yet” in the lists until the first sign-in. Whoever is connected with ORCID and has forgotten the password signs in again with ORCID and sets a new one under “Forgotten your password?”, without the old one. A second factor can be switched on as well.
After the first sign-in, whether with ORCID, through an invitation link or through an admin's link, a **setup assistant** leads once through four steps: details and language, second way to sign in, own AI access (as under Connections) and learning aids. Every step can be skipped, “End setup” ends it altogether; everything is on the account pages afterwards as well.
Admins of an organisation find “Delete account …” on the page of an account under Management > People: the account is blocked at once and deleted after 30 days; until then it can be taken back. Surveys and designs that belong to this person alone pass to a person of the organisation or are deleted (one preset for all, each row changeable). If the person also belongs to another organisation, they are removed with × in the list instead.
Anyone who has an account already does not become a member at once through “Add person” under Management > People: the admin sees an invitation link once (valid for 14 days, for this account only) and gives it to the person, who joins themselves with “Join” while signed in; a guest gets the organisation as home with it. Below the list are the open invitations: ORCID iDs let in without an account, iDs entered before their first sign-in (as admin or in a group) and still valid one-time links of accounts without ORCID, each with its date, who invited and its end; the red bin withdraws an invitation (an account without ORCID stays, only its link ends). Accounts with roles beyond the organisation (admin or member of another organisation, in its groups or surveys) are managed by a platform admin; admins of the organisation can neither block, change or delete them nor take them over by a one-time link, but can remove them from the organisation with ×. Likewise the lead of a course makes one-time links only for course accounts without roles beyond the course. When admins of the organisation change a type under Management > User types, it applies only to accounts they manage: “Carry over to the accounts” leaves out admins of the organisation, accounts at home elsewhere and accounts with roles beyond it, and the platform does not delete such accounts by itself after their end (they are listed as “deletion due” for the platform admins); the type's page gives their number.
Under Management > People the block **People from outside** sets who may bring people who do not belong to the organisation yet into it, its groups and surveys: everyone who may create surveys (the default), only admins of the organisation or only platform admins. Platform admins set the loosest level under Administration > Organisations › Limits and switches; the organisation's admins can only make it stricter. Everyone may still add people of the organisation. Whoever may no longer bring anyone in sees “Create link” and “Invite an address list …” greyed out with the reason; their open join links and invitations by email rest, and “Withdraw all” ends them.
Platform admins create a link under Administration > Researchers in the block **Invitation links** with “Create invitation link …” through which a chosen number of new people register with ORCID (1 to 250), also while open sign-up is off: in the window they choose organisation, user type, period of the accounts (as the user type, 30, 60, 90 or 180 days, 12 months, until a date or without end), how long the link is valid (1 to 90 days) and a note. The period starts with the registration. The link is shown once to copy; the list shows per link organisation, type and period, “3 of 20” used, the end and the accounts that came by it, and “Revoke”. The link changes no existing account and keeps to the organisation's maximum number of accounts.
Under Administration > Organisations › Limits and switches platform admins set whether the members of an organisation see each other by **name**: visible (the default), hidden or “organisation admins decide” (then under Management > People in the block **Names of members**). Hidden means: whoever is at home in the organisation appears to other researchers everywhere as “Member 12” (a fixed number per organisation; outside it with its short name), without ORCID iD and without email address, and cannot be found by name in the people search. Names are seen by the person themselves, admins of the organisation and of the platform and the leads of their groups in the organisation; “Account” says which member others see. Participants still see the people responsible for a survey by name, and so does the pre-registration at the start of fieldwork.
Site admins give an account another home organisation on its page under Administration > Researchers (“Change the home”): its admins then manage it, the former home stays a membership, a user type of the former organisation becomes the one of the same name in the new one (else its default type); period, survey types and the right to create stay within the new organisation's limits.
Site admins create a new organisation under Administration > Organisations with the button “New organisation …”, which opens a window like “Add person …”: short name, optionally a full name, a description and a first admin (ORCID iD or email address of an account). An existing group becomes an organisation under Administration > Groups with “Make an organisation …”. The organisations are listed there as a table (search, sorting, 10, 20 or 50 rows): “People” counts accounts, anyone entered who has never signed in stands next to it as “invited”; then home, groups, surveys and admins. The pencil opens the organisation's page in blocks: names, admins (also by ORCID iD or email address, without an account), limits and switches with “Delete organisation” (only when empty), and last “Institution design”.
Under **Institution design** site admins can upload one design file (JSON) each for light and for dark as a template, so that the organisation's admins do not start from nothing; this is optional. If the organisation has no design of that side yet, the template becomes its organisation design; otherwise its admins see “Apply template”.
Under Management > Design an organisation's admins set, besides the logo, the **organisation design**, light and optionally dark: uploaded as a design file, taken over from a colour scheme or one of their designs, or shaped with “Edit” directly in the design editor, with preview and contrast check. It is the organisation's standard: new surveys start with it; with a dark one as well they switch after the device's setting. All members find it at the top of the appearance of their surveys; they can only change their copy. “Download” gives each side as a design file.
On the same page, “Survey types” holds one of four levels per type: “Off” (the type appears nowhere), “View” (shared surveys at most viewed), “Edit” (works on shared surveys, creates none of its own) and “Create” (also creates own surveys of the type). Whoever has “Create” for any type also creates groups and own designs. “Create everywhere” and “Create nowhere” switch all types at “Edit” at once. A group with ““Create” on joining” gives this level once when someone joins; whoever leaves the group keeps it.

## 21. Learning aids

![An open "Why?" fold on an element: the trade-off of answer modes with its source](img/learning-aid.jpg)

**What it is.**
Small "Why?" folds next to methodological decisions: answer mode, scale points, random order, attention checks, speeders, data pool, quotas, weighting, sample size, conjoint tasks, HB or logit, segments, progress bar, length, random assignment, which test.
Each fold names the trade-off in two or three sentences and gives the source.
Every help ("Why?", "More", "How is it computed?", "What is counted here?") stands as a link with a small "i"; a click opens it as a popup below, a long explanation that would be taller than about half the window as a side panel on the right, on a phone as a sheet from below. A click beside it, Esc or × closes it; printed pages show them written out.
The **glossary of statistics** explains every figure (n, M, SD, CI, α, ω, d, r, p, RSI, part-worth, …) with an example; the question marks next to figures lead there. Below it every “Why?” is listed as “Decisions”, in the groups Plan, Collect and Analysis.
Elements that are hard to build (side-by-side matrix, drill-down, cloze, IAT, delay discounting, association, mark an image) explain how they are built under “How it works” in the editor, with a small example.
On an input field the question mark stands beside the label, not inside it, so the field keeps its name for screen readers.

![The glossary of statistics](img/stats-glossary.jpg)

**Why.**
Askurious is also a teaching tool: students learn the reasons where they make the decision, not in a separate manual.
The aids are on for new accounts and can be switched off on Account › Display and language once they are no longer needed (decision of 2026-10-05).
