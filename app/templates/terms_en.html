Terms of use for researchers

Version: 2026-10-23c (English translation of the German version 2026-10-23c)

These terms apply to all researchers who create or edit surveys on this platform with an account (sign-in with an ORCID iD or with an email address and password). How the platform processes the data of your account is set out in the privacy policy.

1. Operator and purpose

{% include "terms/_operator_en.html" %} It may only be used for research and teaching, not for advertising, address trading or surveys without a scientific purpose.

2. Responsibility for your own surveys

You are responsible for the content, research ethics and data protection of your surveys. This includes in particular an accurate consent form, an ethics approval where required, and compliance with the rules of your institution. Your consent form states everything your survey collects and to whom it passes it on, including the functions and services these terms name. For many elements and settings the standard text adds this by itself, for others the platform offers a paragraph; if you change the text, make sure it stays complete. All persons entered as “Researchers involved” have the same rights to a survey.

Accounts can be limited in time (user type, for example thesis, internship or course, also when registering through an invitation link of the platform, then from the registration on): at the end of the period the platform blocks the account after prior notices and deletes it after the deletion period of the user type if nothing has to be handed over. For accounts with a supervisor, the supervising person is admin of every survey you create. Platform admins can set the period, the types of survey and the creation of own surveys per account.

Your account belongs to an organisation (for example a chair, institute or company on the platform), its home, and can belong to further ones. Groups share only within their organisation, and the people search finds only people of one's own organisations and of shared surveys; whoever you invite to a survey in person sees that survey, also from another organisation, and whoever comes in only that way is a guest without an organisation of their own. Admins of your home organisation can set the user type, the period, the types of survey and the creation of own surveys for your account, block it and manage the organisation's groups, each at most within the limits the platform admins set; they do not see the content or answers of your surveys through this. If your account belongs to this organisation only, they can mark it for deletion: it is then blocked at once and deleted after 30 days unless they take this back; surveys and designs that belong to you alone then pass to a person of the organisation or are deleted. If you also belong to another organisation, they can only remove you from this organisation. The platform admins or the organisation's admins can set that only admins bring people from outside into the organisation, its groups and surveys; everyone may then still add people of the organisation. The admins of an organisation can upload its logo and set a default design for new surveys; section 7 applies to logos.

Participants can delete their answers themselves: via the privacy page of each survey, in the browser used for the participation for up to 15 minutes after it ended, and after that with the deletion code, provided the survey issues one. If a deletion request reaches you directly, you carry it out on the “Export” page under “Deletion requests”. Files that you downloaded or published before a deletion still contain the deleted data; you replace them as far as this is possible. The “Export” page shows whether anything has been deleted since an export. Interviewees of an interview guide do not delete their interviews themselves: they contact you with their deletion code or their identifier, and you delete the interview together with the audio recording and transcript on the “Interviews” page of the guide. You keep and destroy questionnaires on paper yourself, also when a person has their answers deleted; the platform then deletes the participation and the scans and tells you the number of the sheet to destroy. The platform deletes the scans after the takeover (by default after 30 days).

Each survey has retention periods, counted from the end of data collection, which you choose in the settings and which the consent states: one for identifiers from the invitation link and for contact details (new surveys: 6 months), and one for all participations (new surveys: 10 years). When a period expires, the platform deletes without asking again. The platform is not an archive: you are responsible for backing up your research data yourself, for example by exporting or publishing them. Backup copies of the database and of the uploaded files also contain your data: regular backups (every four hours) are deleted after 14 days, backups before an update that rebuilds the database after 30 days at most; the privacy policy names exceptions. Once real participations exist, the periods can only be shortened. For interviews of an interview guide, the periods run from the end of the respective interview, not from the end of data collection: one for audio recordings (new guides: until the transcription has been checked) and one for all other information including the pseudonymised transcripts (new guides: 10 years).

Notices of illegal content

Every survey has the button “File a complaint” on its privacy page, public dashboards a link “Report content”; the legal notice names the point of contact under the Digital Services Act. The platform admins check every notice, for example of infringed copyright, and can remove an image, a file or a text, pause or suspend a survey and suspend an account. The admins of the survey learn of such a measure by email, where it is set up, with its reasons (Art. 17 DSA); you can contest it with the operator via the legal notice, and recourse to the courts remains open.

Processing on your behalf

Insofar as your surveys contain personal data, the operator processes them on your behalf (Art. 28 GDPR) and only on your instructions. This concerns the details of the participants, interviewees and invited persons of your surveys (answers, recordings, transcripts, identifiers and contact details), as long as your surveys exist and until their periods have expired. You give your instructions through the functions of the platform: collecting, analysing, exporting, deleting and the retention periods. Excepted is the pooled analysis under section 4, for which the operator is itself the controller. The operator

  • obliges all persons with access to the server to confidentiality,
  • takes appropriate technical and organisational protective measures (Art. 32 GDPR), including encrypted transmission, access to the administration only for the platform admins after their sign-in (with an ORCID iD, or with a password and a code from an authenticator app), and backups of the server,
  • engages further processors only for operation and for the functions in section 6, binds them to the same data protection obligations and informs you in advance of new or replaced ones; you can object to such a change and, if no agreement is reached, export and delete the surveys concerned,
  • tells you if he considers an instruction to be unlawful,
  • supports you with requests from participants and with your further obligations under Art. 32 to 36 GDPR, and notifies you of data protection breaches without undue delay,
  • hands the data over to you through the export until they are deleted, and deletes them in line with your periods or when you delete the survey,
  • demonstrates compliance with these obligations to you on request and allows inspections by arrangement.

If your institution requires a separate data processing agreement, please contact the operator.

3. Anonymity

The platform's database does not store IP addresses or browser fingerprints. The server's logs record no requests with IP addresses; only messages of the web server about severe errors may contain an IP address, and the logs are overwritten continuously. If you switch on “Estimate country and federal state from the IP address” for a survey, the IP address is looked up once, together with the consent to participate, in a table on the server; only the country and, in Germany, the federal state are stored, never the address. You use the federal state only as what it is, an estimate. The element “Location” records the position of the device after the participant has released it in their browser; exact coordinates can point to a home. You use it only if your research question needs the location, and choose the coarsest level that suffices for this. Surveys with a map load map images from third-party servers, see section 6. You undertake not to identify participants via free texts, URL parameters or other means, unless the consent expressly points this out.

Interviews are not anonymous: the voice and content of an audio recording, notes and transcripts can allow conclusions about the interviewee. You obtain the consent to the interview and to the audio recording yourself, in writing or orally on the recording; the platform only records the form in which this was done. You make an audio recording only if everyone who speaks in the conversation has consented: the interviewee, you or the interviewers and anyone else in the room (section 201 German Criminal Code); you confirm this at the start of every interview with the tick “Consent of everyone who speaks given”. If you upload a recording from a video conference (e.g. Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Webex, BigBlueButton), you follow your institution's rules for these services, upload the sound only and delete the copy at the provider and on your computer as soon as it is on the platform. You do not write names in notes, and you pseudonymise transcripts before passing them on or publishing them. With an interviewer link (the “Interviews” page of the guide) people without an account conduct interviews on your behalf: you choose these people, oblige them to confidentiality and instruct them (Art. 29 and Art. 32(4) GDPR). The link alone gives access to that person's interviews including audio recordings and transcripts, until they hand the transcript over as checked: you give it only to the person themselves, not to group chats or several people, and block or delete the access when the work ends or a device is lost.

Diary studies (experimental) are pseudonymous: the entries of one person are linked by a random person identifier, together with the times of the prompts and the time zone of their phone. You write the texts of the notifications so that they do not give away the topic; the lock screen does not show them, but once unlocked anyone holding the phone reads them. You keep the number and length of the prompts as small as your research question allows, and obtain an ethics approval for intensive data collection or distressing topics. You do not pass the test code of a study on to participants.

4. Validated constructs and pooled analysis (meta-analyses)

The construct collection provides validated scales and standard questions. If the same constructs are used in many surveys, their quality and their relationships can be examined across studies. Therefore the following applies to every survey that uses a construct from the collection:

  • You agree that the operator merges the answers to the construct items with the answers of other surveys and analyses them for scientific purposes, for example for meta-analyses, reliability and validity studies, measurement invariance, norm values and the further development of the construct collection. You warrant that you are entitled to give this agreement for your survey. For this pooled analysis, the operator is itself the controller under data protection law.
  • Only the following are included: the answers to construct items (modified constructs are marked with their deviations), the answers to demographic constructs of the same participation, the language of the survey, the month of participation and the version of the construct.
  • Surveys and participations receive substitute identifiers so that answers to different constructs of the same participation can be linked. The data are thereby pseudonymised: without the operator’s key, neither the survey nor the participant can be determined from the identifiers.
  • Not included are: other questions, free texts, stored URL parameters, technical information, data from test runs and dummy data, and abandoned participations. Also not included are participations flagged by the quality assurance of your survey and participations whose participants state that their answers should not be analysed.
  • The pooled analysis only begins once the data collection of your survey has been completed.
  • Deleted answers drop out of the pooled analysis, also after the retention periods of your survey have expired. Merged files that the operator creates for an analysis are kept only as long as the analysis and its verifiability require, for ten years at most.
  • Results are published only in aggregated form or as a merged data set without assignment to individual surveys. Individual surveys and their researchers are named only with their consent.
  • The consent form of every survey with constructs from the collection shows, below your text, a fixed paragraph about this that you cannot remove.
  • If you give participants data protection information of your own besides the consent form (for example of your institution or from an ethics application), mention the pooled analysis there as well. A paragraph for this is in Settings › Consent and data protection.

If you do not want this, do not use constructs from the collection and create the questions yourself.

The services are intended for moderate use and may fail or limit access without prior notice.

Do not enter any personal data there, and check the results of the AI before you use them. The AI help for categorising open answers and the AI reading of handwriting on paper sheets, by contrast, send answer texts and image sections of participants to OpenAI, which is then a further processor; OpenAI does not use them for training and does not store them for later retrieval. You use these two functions only if the consent or privacy notice of your survey names the transfer to this recipient; the owner or an admin of the survey confirms this before the first use, per recipient.
  • Own AI access: under Account → AI usage you can store a key of your own, for example for KI:connect.nrw or OpenAI. Your AI text functions then run through this provider and under its terms; if you send answer texts of participants there, those terms must allow passing them on. Images are still created by OpenAI through the platform's access. There you can also switch generative AI off for yourself altogether, transcription included. The key is stored encrypted and deleted with “Remove” or with your account.
  • Transcription of interviews: if a transcription server is set up, audio recordings go there to be transcribed. The operator runs the server itself or has it run on its behalf; recordings do not go to OpenAI or other AI providers.
  • Further services for operation: a mail server engaged for emails, also for your invitations to participants; notices to the admins in the Slack service (a provider based in the USA) about complaints, feedback and new accounts (with name and organisation), without contact details and email addresses; timestamps from the DFN association for seal chains and preregistrations, to which only a check value goes; the operator's compute node for costly estimations, which receives answers only with substitute identifiers and only in working memory.
  • ORCID, Zenodo and Google Play: signing in with an ORCID iD and publishing via Zenodo take place through your own account with these services.   • AI agent interface (experimental): with a personal token (page “Account”) you connect an AI agent of your choice with the platform through the Model Context Protocol. Your agent transfers this content to the AI provider you chose for it; you are responsible for this yourself. Do not write personal data into questionnaire texts. A token is valid for 90 days and can be revoked at any time.
  • AI chat for participants (experimental): with the element “AI chat” participants write with an AI model. The messages go through the model access you enter for the survey yourself (provider, address, key, model), never through the platform's access; the provider is your processor, and you need the necessary agreement with it and its permission for this use. You may use the AI chat only with a consent that names it (the standard consent does and asks for a tick of its own), and you are responsible for the instructions to the model and its answers.
