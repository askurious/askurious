Participants

Answers to questionnaires are stored without a name and without an identifier of the person (for interviews, see below); whether they are anonymous depends on what a survey asks: free texts or rare combinations of answers can point to a person. By default, the platform stores no IP addresses, no browser or device identifiers and no tracking cookies. To protect against automated mass requests, the server counts new participations, live votes, complaints and the size of voice recordings per sender for a few minutes up to an hour (complaints for a day) for all its processes together in the database, using an irreversible hash of the IP address (for IPv6 also of its wider network range) whose key changes daily; these counter rows contain no IP address, are not logged and are deleted after one day at the latest. In the same way it counts confirmations of receipt of complaints per email address for one day, using such a hash of the address. Failed attempts with codes and secrets (such as deletion, survey or diary codes, sign-ins, invitation, join and interviewer links) and the devices of one network voting on a question of a live vote are counted by the server for all its processes together in the database, likewise only by such a hash, whose key changes daily; it deletes these counter rows after about an hour. The web server also slows down too many requests per address and keeps the addresses for this in working memory only. Certain surveys may nevertheless activate individual aspects of these (except tracking cookies), but then expressly point this out at the beginning. Some surveys record coarse technical information (device type, operating system and browser without version numbers, window width in classes); the consent of the survey concerned points this out. A technically necessary session cookie only records your progress within the survey. If a live vote asks quiz questions, a nickname is stored with the participation for the leaderboard, chosen by the person or assigned at random, and with teams the team as well; it appears on the presentation, is not part of the export and is deleted with the participation. If someone sends an emoji in a live vote, it is counted for the slide shown at that moment, without reference to the person or the device. Questions from the audience are stored with the slide shown when they were sent. If presenters write on presentation slides with a pen, these strokes are stored per run and slide; they contain nothing about participants and are deleted with the run or the poll. If a word cloud allows several answers, the device shows its own words while voting on the question is open; each can be withdrawn there and is then deleted. If a survey contains a voice message, your browser records your voice after you allow the microphone; the recording is kept on the platform's server outside the publicly reachable files, goes to no other service and is deleted with your answers. Voices can identify people; you can type instead if the survey offers it. If a survey contains an AI chat, you write there with an AI assistant. Your messages and its answers are stored in full as data of the study and deleted with your answers. To answer, your messages, without e-mail addresses, web addresses and phone numbers and without an identifier of your participation, go to the AI service the study set up for this itself (never over the platform's access); the consent of the survey names it, and you agree to it with a tick of its own. The page always says that an AI is answering. If a survey shows a video or an audio recording with a playback log, it stores how you played it (start, pause, jumps, which parts played, whether the sound was off and whether the window was in the background); camera and microphone are not used. If a survey loads simple blocks in one piece (“Page through faster, even on a weak connection”), your browser keeps the answers of the block you are working on briefly in the session storage of the tab (sessionStorage) until they are sent; this storage ends with the tab.

You can delete your answers yourself, without giving reasons: via “Delete answers” on the privacy page of the survey concerned (link at the bottom of each of its pages). In the browser in which you took part, this is possible while answering and up to 15 minutes after completing the participation without further details; after that, and on other devices, with the deletion code from the last page of the survey, provided it issues one. For this, the platform only records how many participations of a survey were deleted on which day. Whatever has been analysed, downloaded or published until then cannot be retrieved.

Password reset requests, including the entered address, are temporarily stored encrypted and processed outside the request, including when no account exists for the address; the job is valid for one hour and is then deleted during the next maintenance pass. Newly self-chosen sign-in addresses are activated only after email confirmation. Until confirmation, the address, a hash of the confirmation link and the encrypted password hash are stored. The job is valid for 24 hours and is then deleted during the next maintenance pass; no plaintext password. Existing and administratively created access remains usable.

AI categorisation removes recognisable email addresses, telephone numbers and URLs before transmission. AI translation uses local placeholders for these contact details and known names and institutions of the survey team; they are restored only on the server. This is not complete anonymisation of free text.

Surveys have retention periods counted from the end of their data collection: identifiers from the invitation link and separately stored information (e.g. an email address for a prize draw) are deleted after the first period, all answers later. A live poll is never closed; for it the period for the answers counts from the last participation. Pooled analysis of validated constructs (meta-analyses)

If a survey contains validated constructs from the area “Constructs” (e.g. standardised scales or the standard questions on age and gender), the operator of the platform (see “Privacy: controller”) combines the answers to them with answers from other surveys after the survey has ended and analyses them for scientific purposes, such as meta-analyses, reliability and validity studies, norm values and the further development of the constructs. The operator is itself responsible for this, not the researchers of the individual survey. Only the answers to these constructs are included, together with the answers to the standard questions on age and gender of the same participation (not the other standard demographic questions such as education, income or origin), the language of the survey, the month of participation and the version of the construct; no other questions, no free-text answers, no identifiers from the invitation link and no technical details. Surveys and participations are given substitute identifiers (pseudonymised); only the operator holds the key.

The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR); the consent form of each survey names this analysis in a fixed paragraph. If you delete your answers, they also leave this analysis; what has been analysed or published by then cannot be taken back. The operator keeps combined files for at most ten years. Only summarised results or data sets that cannot be traced to a single survey are published.

Interviewees

For interviews with an interview guide, the researchers store on the platform what they record during the conversation: an identifier (e.g. “I03”), date and form of the interview, the type of consent, notes and time stamps per guiding question, ticks for which aspects came up, which questions were asked and whether a specific question applied, the results of tasks in the conversation (such as a ranking or sorted cards) with the time they were entered, short questionnaire and postscript, and, if desired, an audio recording and the transcript. Interviews are therefore not anonymous: your voice and what you said can allow conclusions about you. The researchers are to avoid writing names in notes and are to pseudonymise transcripts. The researchers who conduct the interview are responsible; they obtain your consent in advance, in writing or orally on the recording.

If the interview takes place in a video conference (e.g. Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Webex, BigBlueButton), the researchers can upload the audio recording made there to the platform. Only sound is accepted, no picture; the platform notes what it was recorded with and, if a file holds one voice only, whether it is that of the interviewer or of the interviewee. Everyone who speaks in the conversation must have consented before a recording. The researchers are responsible for the recording at the video conference provider and its deletion there. Alternatively the interviewer's browser can record the sound of the video conference (a shared tab or screen) directly: only the sound is stored, mixed with the microphone, like a recording in the interview; the picture is not stored, and nothing goes to the video conference provider.

Interviews can also be conducted by people without an account whom the researchers engage with a personal link (interviewers). They see only their own interviews with recording and transcript, each only until they hand the transcript over as checked; the researchers remain responsible.

For some tasks in the interview the interviewee can open a page on their own device (participant screen), through a link or QR code the interviewers make per interview. The page shows only the task or the short questionnaire (details about the person) the interviewers release at that moment, nothing of the guide, the notes or the recording; answers to the short questionnaire are stored like those entered there, with the note that they came from the participant screen. Only the result of the task is stored, as part of the interview, with the time on the interview clock and the note that it was given on the participant screen and, if so, changed by the interviewers; besides that, of the link only a hash, a random device identifier of the connection and the time of the last request, no details of the device and no IP address. The browser holds only a technically necessary session cookie. The link opens on one device only and holds for 30 minutes unopened; the connection ends with the interview, on disconnecting or with a new link, after 6 hours at the latest. Failed attempts with a link are counted like other failed attempts (see above).

Audio recordings are kept on the platform’s server outside the publicly accessible files; only researchers who may edit the interview guide and the interviewers of the respective interview can listen to them. For transcription, the recording is transmitted to a transcription server that the operator runs itself or has run on its behalf; recordings, notes and transcripts are not passed to OpenAI or other AI providers.

Interviewers without an account

Anyone who conducts interviews with an interviewer link needs no account. Stored are the name or initials entered by the researchers, the kind of devices on which the link was opened (e.g. “iPad”) and the time of last use; of the link itself only a hash. The browser holds only a technically necessary session cookie; it ends after 60 minutes without activity, on signing out and when the researchers block the access. What has not reached the server yet during an interview (pieces of the audio recording, notes, answers in the short questionnaire and postscript), for instance on a poor connection, is kept by the browser of the interview device in its local storage until the server confirms it; then it is deleted. So that the interview view opens again without a connection, the browser also keeps a copy of that page (with the interview's notes) and the state of the interview until the interview is ended, at most 12 hours after its last use; on request, waiting audio pieces can be saved as a file on the device and read in later. In offline mode (experimental, switched on per guide) the server reserves interviews with identifier and deletion code for one device in advance; the interviews the device conducts without a network (notes, answers, audio recording, the deletion codes) are kept by its browser encrypted with a PIN until they are transferred, at most seven days, and deleted afterwards; a deletion code works even before the transfer. Failed attempts to open a link are counted like other failed attempts (see above), only by a hash of the address whose key changes daily. The purpose is conducting the interviews on behalf of the researchers, who are responsible for it. The access is deleted when the researchers delete it or the guide; the interviews keep the name of who conducted them.

Questionnaires on paper

If you fill in a questionnaire on paper, the researchers scan the sheet and upload the scans to the platform. Askurious reads your ticks on its own server; the researchers type in handwriting. The scans go to no other service; only if the researchers have an AI read the handwriting and told you so in the consent, sections of the writing fields (without your other answers and without the number of the sheet) go to the AI service the researchers use over their own AI access, never over the platform's access. Every sheet carries a random number that says nothing about you; please do not write your name on the sheet. Without a tick at the consent, the sheet is not accepted and its scan is deleted at once.

The scans are kept outside the publicly accessible files and are deleted after the takeover (as the researchers choose: at once, after 30 days or with the answers), and also when you have your answers deleted and with the periods of the survey. Deletion: if the survey gives deletion codes, yours is on a page of its own after the sheet, which you keep; with it you delete your answers yourself, also before the sheet is scanned. Otherwise contact the researchers with the number of your sheet; they delete answers and scans and destroy the paper sheet. Without the number, an anonymous sheet cannot be found (Art. 11 GDPR).

Diary studies with the app

In a diary study you join with the app “Askurious” for Android (QR code or join code) and answer short questions over days, weeks or with no fixed end: at fixed times after a notification, or whenever you want to report something. The app needs no account and sends no device identifier. The study consent explains which personal data the questions collect. When you join, the platform creates a random key per study; the app keeps it on the phone (not in backups and not when moving to a new device), the platform only a check value of it that cannot be reversed. Before every entry the app clears the cookies and the storage of its questionnaire view, so the platform cannot link two studies on one phone. Input drafts may temporarily remain on the phone. Answers given without a connection are kept until the server confirms storage or you remove the study from the phone.

The app also keeps the diary survey summary, diary survey contact and transfer status on the device. A bounded draft of ordinary input fields is kept in Android saved instance state for recovery; it is not submitted as an online answer or included in cloud backups. Unsubmitted recordings and drawings cannot reliably be recovered after a process restart. Microphone or location access is used only for a relevant question after your permission; the diary survey consent explains the data sent. There is no background recording or background location collection.

Your entries in a study are linked by a random person identifier so that the researchers can analyse changes over time; they are therefore pseudonymous, not anonymous. For each entry the platform stores, besides the answers, the part of the study, the number of the entry, the day of the study, the time of the prompt and when the phone showed it, the start and end of the entry (for an entry answered without a connection, what the phone says), when the platform received the entry, a random identifier the app gives each answer (so that an answer sent twice is stored once), and the time zone of the phone; for the person the period of the study, their status (for example “left”) and when they left the study, language and time zone (after a change also the earlier one, with the day from which the new one applies), the quiet hours they set in the app for random prompts, pauses the researchers set for them, when the app last synchronised and when it last fetched the pages for answers without a connection. If the researchers publish the data through the platform, it replaces the person identifier with a random number that is valid only in that data set.

If you join with a personal code the researchers gave you, the platform stores which code belongs to your person identifier, so that you can connect your participation again with the code, for example on a new phone; the earlier phone then loses access. This works only after you have contacted the researchers and they have released the code; the release holds once and for two hours. Anyone who knows who was given which code can link the entries to the person.

For offline pages, the platform stores their version and random order, associated with your participation, for 30 days. Invalid or incompatible submissions are retained for review by researchers and excluded from regular analyses. A correction retains the original, clarification record, time and editor. These data follow the survey’s retention periods; deleting your answers also removes these data.

On the lock screen, notifications (and, if the researchers wish, one reminder while a question is open) only show “A short question is waiting”; the title and text of the study appear only after unlocking. “Leave study” ends the questions; your answers so far stay with the researchers. “Delete my answers” in the app deletes all entries of the study on the platform, as does the deletion code at the end of the first questionnaire. Anyone who does not finish the first questionnaire is deleted after 7 days together with what they began. The entries are subject to the retention periods of the study like any survey. The researchers who run a study are responsible for it. You download the app from Google Play, where Google’s terms and privacy policy apply. The app itself only sends to the participants’ address of the platform. It shows the questionnaires in Android System WebView, a part of Android that Google provides and updates through Google Play. The WebView may contact Google services on its own: Safe Browsing checks the addresses opened against known harmful sites (as a rule only shortened hashes of the addresses go to Google), and while an autofill service is switched on on the phone, it asks content-autofill.googleapis.com for predictions for the form fields; Google then receives characteristics of the form (not your answers) and the phone’s IP address. The app keeps the questionnaires out of the autofill service and has switched off the WebView’s usage statistics to Google; it cannot switch off the WebView’s prediction requests. You avoid them by switching off the autofill service in the Android settings. Google’s privacy policy applies to these requests.

Access permissions for diary questionnaires are stored as hashes, are valid for twelve hours and work only in the questionnaire view that opened them first; expired hashes are deleted during the next maintenance run. Leaving, pausing, ending and reconnecting the app revoke old questionnaire links and browser access. A newly available exit questionnaire remains possible. Already stored answers retain the diary survey’s retention periods.

Server logs and further services

Only messages of the web server about severe errors may contain an IP address. The logs serve only to find faults and are overwritten continuously: the server keeps at most 60 MB of each service (three files of 20 MB) and deletes older entries. For the security of the accounts the log also records: sign-ins and failed sign-ins, pauses and locks after failed attempts, changes of the password and of the second factor, one-time links made and used, and invitations and join links accepted, each with the time, the number of the account and a hash of the network of the request whose key changes daily (no IP address), without passwords, codes or contents; also, per minute, how often rate limits applied. These entries are in the same logs and are overwritten in the same way.
